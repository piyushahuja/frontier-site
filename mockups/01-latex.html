Strategic enterprises from frontier science

India's future world leaders are already here. We connect them — early — to people a few steps ahead, and give them permission to build something of genuine global consequence.

§1  Manifesto

India keeps only about a fifth of the value of the large enterprises its people build.1 Over the last decade, long-term ownership capital arrived, plateaued, and almost none of it reached the frontier of science. It came to rent talent, not to own what that talent creates.1 Ownership, not employment, is where value compounds. A country can supply the world's researchers and still keep little of what they make.

Consider the scale of what is missed.

NVIDIA ≈ all of listed India
$4.9 trillion ≈ $5 trillion
One company is now worth about as much as every publicly listed company in India combined.
As of July 2026. Sources: company market cap; BSE aggregate.

That is the gap concentrated excellence closes: not many good companies — a few of genuine global consequence.

A nation that only imports the frontier cannot help steer it. A country that consumes technology built elsewhere is a client, not a partner. Self-reliance — atmanirbharta — was the first step. The frontier company is where self-reliance matures into contribution: to be a friend the world actually needs, India must be a maker at the frontier, not merely a market for it.

A six-year ladder

  • Year 2 Exposure. Students meet people building at the frontier, and get permission to think in decades.
  • Year 3 Projects. They begin real research inside the community.
  • Year 4 Publish. The work reaches the frontier — NeurIPS and its peers.
  • +1–2 yrs Found. Companies spin out.
  • Then Frontier. One becomes a company of genuine global consequence.

§2  Lecture Series

On campus, weekly, Monsoon semester

At IIT Delhi's Department of Computer Science, alumni ten to twenty years ahead return to tell the truth about the decisions they made — what they got right, what they got wrong, what they wish they had known. Not placement talks; first-person reflection. Convened with Prof. Naveen Garg, Head of Department and Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar laureate.Six tracks over twelve weeks: Academia & Research · Research→Venture · Industry & Product · Capital & Investing · Public Service · Alternative Paths.

Speakers include Savin Goyal (Outerbounds), Ananya Agarwal (Neo Labs), Raghu Mahajan (ICTS), and others.
