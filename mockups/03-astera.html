Strategic enterprises from frontier science

India's future world leaders are already here. We connect them — early — to people a few steps ahead, and give them permission to build something of genuine global consequence.

Manifesto

India keeps only about a fifth of the value of the large enterprises its people build. Over the last decade, long-term ownership capital arrived, plateaued, and almost none of it reached the frontier of science. It came to rent talent, not to own what that talent creates.

NVIDIA ≈ all of listed India
$4.9 trillion  ≈  $5 trillion
One company · every listed Indian company combined · as of July 2026

That is the gap concentrated excellence closes: not many good companies — a few of genuine global consequence.

A nation that only imports the frontier cannot help steer it. A country that consumes technology built elsewhere is a client, not a partner. Self-reliance — atmanirbharta — was the first step. The frontier company is where self-reliance matures into contribution: to be a friend the world actually needs, India must be a maker at the frontier, not merely a market for it.

Lecture Series

On campus, weekly, through the Monsoon semester

At IIT Delhi's Department of Computer Science, alumni ten to twenty years ahead return to tell the truth about the decisions they made — what they got right, what they got wrong, what they wish they had known. Not placement talks: first-person reflection. Convened with Prof. Naveen Garg, Head of Department and Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar laureate.
